PDF remediation before and after: three city documents

Samples · Last reviewed November 2026 · Sources linked inline

These are three real public documents from Minnesota cities, remediated on our own initiative to show our work. They are not client work. For each one you can download the original, our final PDF, and the veraPDF PDF/UA-1 report we generated with the free, open-source validator. All three finals pass PDF/UA-1 machine validation and were checked by hand for reading order, headings, tables and alternative text.

1. City council agenda (untagged original)

The original agenda had no tags at all, and the attachment lists repeated at every page break. The final has real headings and lists, alternative text on the city logo, each list appears once, and all 30 attachment links still open the same city files as the original.

  • Original agenda (PDF)
  • Remediated agenda (PDF)
  • veraPDF PDF/UA-1 report for the remediated agenda

2. Special meeting agenda with Spanish and Hmong passages

The original was tagged but failed PDF/UA-1. The final has headings and lists, the Spanish and Hmong passages are marked by language so screen readers switch pronunciation, and the email link has a descriptive name.

  • Original special meeting agenda (PDF)
  • Remediated special meeting agenda (PDF)
  • veraPDF PDF/UA-1 report for the remediated special meeting agenda

3. Fee schedule (tables)

The original is three Letter-size pages; it was tagged but failed PDF/UA-1, and its 19 tables marked every header as a column header (131 of them) with no row headers. The remediated file has 90 row headers and 41 column headers, so each fee is tied to its name and amount in the tag structure, including four tables that continue across a page break. We checked that structure in the tags and with veraPDF; we have not tested it with a screen reader.

Two things to know about this sample: the remediated file came out as six A4 pages (for a US client we export on Letter, matching the original), and it adds text the original did not have: "None listed" in five empty cells, a completed word in brackets ("[W]ater"), park names on four refund rows, and three section headings. On a client job, additions like these are listed for the city to approve before anything is published, and typos in the source are flagged rather than silently fixed.

What every remediation includes

  • Tag structure: headings in order, real lists, tables with header cells, figures with alternative text, decorative content marked as artifacts.
  • Document title, language, and language marking for passages in other languages.
  • A veraPDF PDF/UA-1 report with every file, plus a short note on anything a person at your office needs to decide.
