How to inventory your website's PDFs before the ADA Title II deadline

How-to · Last reviewed November 2026 · Sources linked inline

Before a city, county, district or agency can budget for Title II document work, it needs to know what it has posted. You can build a usable inventory yourself in an afternoon or two. Here is a method that works with tools you already have.

1. Collect the PDF links

  • Your CMS first. Most government website platforms (CivicPlus Document Center, Granicus, Revize, WordPress media library) can list or export uploaded files. Start there; it is the most complete source for your own site.
  • Your sitemap. Open yoursite.gov/sitemap.xml. Some sitemaps list documents directly.
  • A site search. Searching a web search engine for site:yoursite.gov filetype:pdf finds indexed PDFs, though not all of them.
  • Separate portals. List your agenda system (Legistar, CivicClerk, BoardDocs, Simbli and similar), records portal (Laserfiche WebLink) and any third-party sites you post to. Their documents count too, and website-only counts miss them.

2. Record a few facts per file

A spreadsheet with one row per PDF is enough. Record the URL, department, document type (agenda, minutes, form, budget, report, notice), page count, last-modified date and whether it is used to apply for or get a service. Adobe Acrobat shows page count and whether a file is tagged (File, Properties, "Tagged PDF"); a file with no selectable text is a scan and needs OCR first.

3. Sort into what to fix, archive or remove

  • Fix: forms, applications and anything people use to get a service, plus current documents.
  • Archive: older documents kept only for reference can be moved to a clearly marked archive section. The rule's archive exception has conditions (created before the compliance date, kept for reference, research or recordkeeping, not changed afterward, stored in a dedicated archive area).
  • Remove: duplicates and documents nobody needs.

4. Turn it into a budget

Multiply the pages to fix by a realistic rate. Public contract prices for ordinary pages cluster around $3.50 to $6 per page, with forms, complex layouts and scans costing more; see what PDF remediation costs, or plug your counts into the free cost estimator. If you are writing an RFP, state the page counts and the mix (short forms versus long reports) so vendors can price it.

If you'd rather not do it by hand

Our PDF backlog sizing ($149) does steps 1 and 2 automatically for one website: PDF links found, an estimated page range, which sampled files are untagged or scanned and when each was last changed, one row per analyzed file. It covers the entity's own site only, so add your separate portals to it.

This page is practical guidance, not legal advice. The rule itself is 28 CFR part 35, subpart H.
