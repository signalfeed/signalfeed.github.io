Title II PDF Backlog Index: US public universities

Data · Last reviewed November 2026 · Sources linked inline

Public universities are covered by the ADA Title II web rule (28 CFR part 35, subpart H). Most are part of state government, which puts them on the April 26, 2027 compliance date: web content, including PDFs, must conform to WCAG 2.1 Level AA, subject to the rule's exceptions (check your own institution's status). We measured what that means for the documents public universities post: catalogs, policies, forms, board materials and reports. See also the city and county Backlog Index.

What we found

  • 251 of 501 institutions measured (the rest blocked automated visitors, host their documents elsewhere, or had too few PDFs to sample; they are reported as "insufficient sample", never as clean).
  • 12,556 PDFs analyzed. 26.7% were untagged, which means a screen reader gets no structure at all, and 4.5% were scanned, image-only pages with no usable text.
  • 9 of 752 sampled PDFs passed the veraPDF PDF/UA-1 checks.
  • New documents too: of 5,221 sampled PDFs last modified in 2025 or 2026, 19.7% were untagged. Setting up accessible export now helps keep the backlog from growing.
  • The median measured institution has roughly 405 pages of PDFs failing at least one basic check (untagged, no document language, or scanned). Across all measured institutions that is on the order of 1,200,000 pages. This is a sample-based estimate, and a few very long documents can inflate an individual institution's number.

What this is, and what it isn't

These are automated signals, not a conformance evaluation. An automated check can show that a PDF definitely fails (for example, it has no tags). It can never show that a PDF conforms: that takes human review, including with assistive technology. Treat the numbers as the size of the problem, not as a verdict on any institution.

Not every PDF has to be fixed. The rule has exceptions for archived web content and for preexisting conventional electronic documents, unless they are currently used to apply for, access or participate in a service. We record how recently each document was modified so that this can be judged; the estimates above do not subtract exempt documents.

Method

  • Targets: public 4-year institutions with 5,000+ students and a website in the federal IPEDS directory, 2024 (501 institutions). We measure the main website only; departmental and course-management systems are not covered.
  • Crawler: obeys robots.txt, honors crawl delays (correction, Nov 27, 2026: until then it capped them at 10 seconds, so 53 of about 3,600 sites that asked for a longer delay got one request every 10 seconds; it now honors the full delay or skips the site), makes at most one request per second per site, identifies itself honestly, never logs in, and stops at any bot challenge. It reads up to 150 pages and the site's sitemaps, then samples up to 60 PDFs per institution.
  • Checks per PDF: tags (structure tree), document language, title, scanned/image-only pages, page count and modification date. A small sample per institution also gets veraPDF PDF/UA-1 validation.
  • Estimated pages are scaled from the sample to all PDF links found on the pages we could reach. Institutions that keep documents on separate systems (catalog, policy or board portals, learning management systems) are undercounted.
  • Data as of 2026-11-20. We don't collect personal information; contact emails are not stored.

Per-institution data

The workbook lists every targeted institution (measured or not): PDFs found and estimated, untagged and scanned shares, recent-document share, estimated pages needing remediation, a rough market size, and the days left to the compliance date. It's built for remediation firms, consultants and web agencies deciding where to focus, and for agencies benchmarking against their peers.

Per-institution rows are available on request for research and planning: email us with the states you need.
