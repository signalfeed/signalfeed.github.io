PDF backlog sizing for remediation bids

Data · Last reviewed November 2026 · Sources linked inline

Pricing a document-remediation bid for a city or county usually starts with a guess: how many PDFs, how many pages, how much is scanned, how much is forms versus long reports. If the solicitation doesn't state volumes, or states them loosely, the bid carries that risk. This service measures one entity's public PDF backlog so you can price it from data.

What you get

An Excel workbook for one US city or county, built from an automated crawl of its own website:

  • Summary: PDF links found, an estimated page range (low and high), the share of sampled PDFs with no tags, the share that are scanned images with no text layer (OCR first), a veraPDF PDF/UA-1 sample, and how much was modified in 2025 to 2026.
  • Page-count mix: how many files are short (under 10 pages), medium or long (200+), so you can scope the long ones separately.
  • Folders: which sections of the site hold the PDFs (for example planning, council packets, permits).
  • Documents: one row per analyzed PDF with its URL, pages, tagged, scanned, date and size, so you can spot-check anything.
  • README: the method and its limits in plain language.

How it works

  1. Buy on Gumroad ($149) and enter the city or county, plus the RFP number or link if there is one.
  2. We crawl the entity's website politely (robots.txt respected, one request per second) and analyze the PDFs with PyMuPDF and veraPDF.
  3. You get the workbook by email within 3 business days. If the site can't be crawled (blocked, or documents live only behind a search portal), you get a full refund.

Order a sizing ($149)

Limits, stated plainly

  • These are automated signals, not a conformance audit or legal opinion. A tagged PDF is not necessarily accessible.
  • Only the entity's own website is crawled. Documents on other systems (agenda platforms such as Legistar or BoardDocs, Laserfiche WebLink, intranets, records portals behind search forms) are not counted, so treat the result as a floor where those exist. We note any such platforms we see linked.
  • Page totals are estimated from a sample. A few very long files (assessment rolls, budgets, plans) can move the high end a lot, which is why we give a range and list the largest files.
  • If the solicitation states volumes, those take precedence.

The same crawler produces the Title II PDF Backlog Index, which covers 657 cities and the county edition in aggregate. The sizing goes deeper on one entity: a larger page and PDF budget and a bigger veraPDF sample.
