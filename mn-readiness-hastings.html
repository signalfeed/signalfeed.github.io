Title II PDF readiness: Hastings, Minnesota

Data · Last reviewed November 2026 · Sources linked inline

Hastings must make its web content, including posted PDFs, meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA by April 26, 2028 under the ADA Title II web rule. Here is what an automated scan of https://www.hastingsmn.gov/ found.

What stands out

Many PDFs we sampled have no document title. Fill in File > Properties > Title in Word before exporting, so the title rather than a file name is announced.

1 of the PDF links we found sit in a folder that the site's robots.txt asks crawlers not to fetch, so we did not check them and they are not in these numbers.

Method and limits

These are automated checks, not a legal finding and not a WCAG conformance test. In November 2026 a crawler identifying itself as SignalFeedReadinessBot visited up to 300 public HTML pages on each city's main website, obeying robots.txt and making at most one request per second. It collected PDF links on the same website and analyzed up to 400 of them (a random sample where more were found). It did not log in, submit forms, or get around any bot protection; a site that blocked or challenged it is reported as blocked.

For each PDF it recorded the page count, whether the file has a tag structure (a StructTreeRoot), whether a document language and title are set, and whether sampled pages have extractable text (pages that are images only are counted as scanned). Up to 20 PDFs per city were also run through veraPDF against PDF/UA-1, the machine-checkable part of PDF accessibility.

  • Passing these checks does not make a PDF accessible. Reading order, correct tags, table headers and image descriptions need a person.
  • Not every PDF must be fixed. The rule (28 CFR 35.201) has exceptions, including archived content kept only for reference and some preexisting documents. We cannot tell from the outside which files qualify.
  • Coverage is partial: 300 pages is not the whole site, and documents in agenda systems, third-party portals or other domains were not counted.
  • “Estimated pages to remediate” adds up the pages of sampled PDFs that fail at least one basic check (no tags, no language, or scanned) and scales that to all PDF links found. Treat it as an order of magnitude.
