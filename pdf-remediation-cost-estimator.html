PDF remediation cost estimator

Tool · Last reviewed November 2026 · Sources linked inline

Budgeting for the ADA Title II web rule usually starts with one question: roughly what will it cost to fix the PDFs we post? This estimator turns a page count into a cost range using prices from public US government contracts and bids (the same figures as our PDF remediation cost page). It runs in your browser; nothing you type is sent anywhere.

How the estimate works

  • Simple pages are priced at $3.50, $4.12 and $10.00 per page (25th percentile, median and 75th percentile of Washington's 2026 contract ceiling rates for generic documents, simple pages).
  • Complex pages use $4.14, $6.00 and $20.00 per page (the same contract, complex pages).
  • Scanned pages add OCR repair at $0.95, $3.47 and $5.75 per page (federal GPO bids, 2021-22) on top of the tagging price.
  • Washington's rates are maximum rates, not what agencies paid, and GPO figures include losing bids. Scopes and quality checks differ between vendors.

Getting the inputs right

The page count matters most. If you don't know it, this guide explains how to inventory your PDFs with a spreadsheet. Before you multiply, take out what may not need fixing: the rule has exceptions for archived content and for older documents that aren't used to apply for, access or participate in a service. Counting what's in scope first can cut the bill substantially.

Checking a quote

If a vendor's quote is far above the high end here, ask what's included: forms, alt text for complex images, rush turnaround, screen-reader testing. If it's far below the low end, ask how the work is checked.

  • Every price point, with vendor, unit, date and source: PDF Remediation Price Benchmark (Excel), $29.
  • Your own entity's PDFs counted for you (how many, an estimated page range, and for up to 1,500 of them which are untagged or scanned): PDF inventory of one public entity, $149.

This is a budgeting tool, not legal advice or a conformance assessment.
