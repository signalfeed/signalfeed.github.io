Title II PDF accessibility: what we found across five kinds of public entities

Data · Last reviewed November 2026 · Sources linked inline

Under the ADA Title II web rule (28 CFR part 35, subpart H), state and local governments must make web content, including the PDFs they post, conform to WCAG 2.1 Level AA, subject to the rule's exceptions. We measured the PDFs on the public websites of five kinds of public entities: 1,690 entities, 83,243 PDFs. Only 30 of 5,060 sampled PDFs passed a veraPDF PDF/UA-1 check.

What stands out

  • Roughly two in five sampled PDFs on city, county, state and special-district sites have no accessibility tags at all; universities are better at about one in four.
  • Scanned, image-only PDFs are most common on county and special-district sites (about one in five), and they need OCR before any other fix.
  • Almost nothing passes a full PDF/UA-1 check. Tags are necessary but not sufficient.
  • Agendas and records often live on separate systems (Legistar, Granicus, Laserfiche and others) that website-only counts miss, so every number here is a floor.

What this is, and what it isn't

These are automated signals from a polite crawler (robots.txt respected, one request per second per site, no logins), not conformance evaluations or legal findings. Not every PDF has to be fixed: the rule has exceptions for archived web content and for preexisting documents that are not currently used to apply for, access or participate in a service. We publish aggregates only and never name or rank an individual government.
