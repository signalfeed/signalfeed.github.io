Title II PDF Backlog Index: US special district websites

Data · Last reviewed November 2026 · Sources linked inline

Special districts (transit, water, fire, park, library, port and similar districts) are Title II public entities. Under the ADA Title II web rule (28 CFR part 35, subpart H), special district governments have until April 26, 2028 to make web content, including PDFs, conform to WCAG 2.1 Level AA, subject to the rule's exceptions. We measured what that means for the documents districts publish on the websites their .gov domains lead to: board agendas, budgets, plans, rate notices and forms. For cities and counties, see the city and county editions.

What we found

  • 481 of 873 special-district sites measured (the rest blocked automated visitors, host their documents elsewhere, or had too few PDFs to sample; they are reported as "insufficient sample", never as clean).
  • 24,680 PDFs analyzed. 40.0% were untagged, which means a screen reader gets no structure at all, and 19.0% were scanned, image-only pages with no usable text.
  • 2 of 1439 sampled PDFs passed the veraPDF PDF/UA-1 checks.
  • New documents too: of 11,656 sampled PDFs last modified in 2025 or 2026, 31.2% were untagged. Setting up accessible export now helps keep the backlog from growing.
  • The median measured site has roughly 520 pages of PDFs failing at least one basic check (untagged, no document language, or scanned). Across all measured sites that is on the order of 1,000,000 pages. This is a sample-based estimate, and a few very long documents can inflate an individual site's number.

What this is, and what it isn't

These are automated signals, not a conformance evaluation. An automated check can show that a PDF definitely fails (for example, it has no tags). It can never show that a PDF conforms: that takes human review, including with assistive technology. Treat the numbers as the size of the problem, not as a verdict on any district.

Not every PDF has to be fixed. The rule has exceptions for archived web content and for preexisting conventional electronic documents, unless they are currently used to apply for, access or participate in a service. We record how recently each document was modified so that this can be judged; the estimates above do not subtract exempt documents.

Coverage is partial: we started from the .gov domains registered to special districts; some of them forward to the district's main .com or .org site, which is then the site we measured. Districts with no registered .gov domain, and documents on separate agenda or records portals, are not counted, so totals are a floor.

Method

  • Targets: the .gov domains registered as special districts in CISA's public .gov registry (1,178 domains), probed for a working site and merged where several domains lead to the same site (873 sites).
  • Crawler: obeys robots.txt, honors crawl delays (correction, Nov 27, 2026: until then it capped them at 10 seconds, so 53 of about 3,600 sites that asked for a longer delay got one request every 10 seconds; it now honors the full delay or skips the site), makes at most one request per second per site, identifies itself honestly, never logs in, and stops at any bot challenge. It reads up to 150 pages and the site's sitemaps, then samples up to 60 PDFs per site.
  • Checks per PDF: tags (structure tree), document language, title, scanned/image-only pages, page count and modification date. A small sample per site also gets veraPDF PDF/UA-1 validation.
  • Estimated pages are scaled from the sample to all PDF links found on the pages we could reach. Sites that keep documents on separate systems are undercounted.
  • Data as of 2026-11-26. We don't collect personal information; contact emails are not stored.
