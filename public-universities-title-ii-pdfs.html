Public universities and the April 2027 ADA Title II deadline: what to do about PDFs

Regulation · Last reviewed November 2026 · Sources linked inline

Public colleges and universities are covered by Title II of the ADA. Under the Department of Justice's 2024 web rule (28 CFR part 35, subpart H), a public entity's compliance date depends on the population of the government it belongs to. Most public universities are part of state government, which means April 26, 2027; an institution run by a smaller local government (for example some community college districts) may fall under the April 26, 2028 date. Confirm your institution's status with counsel. (The DOJ moved the original dates back by one year in April 2026.)

What counts

The rule covers web content the institution provides or makes available, directly or through contractors. On public websites that means admissions and financial aid forms, catalogs and handbooks, policies, board of trustees or regents materials, budgets and reports, event and facilities documents, and job postings. Course content in a learning management system is also web content the institution makes available. DOJ had proposed special exceptions for password-protected course content, but the final rule did not keep them, so plan for course materials too.

The two exceptions that matter most for public documents are archived web content (created before the compliance date, kept only for reference, research or recordkeeping, not changed after archiving, and stored in a clearly marked archive area) and preexisting conventional electronic documents posted before the compliance date, unless they are currently used to apply for, access or participate in the institution's programs. This page is not legal advice.

Where public universities stand

We measured the PDFs on the public websites of 251 public four-year universities with 5,000 or more students. About 27% of the sampled PDFs had no accessibility tags at all, about 5% were scanned images with no usable text, and 9 of 752 sampled PDFs passed a veraPDF PDF/UA-1 check. Universities are in better shape than cities and counties on tagging, but the volume is large. State-level numbers are on the public university Backlog Index. These are automated signals, not conformance findings, and they cover public pages only, not course sites.

A practical order of work

  1. Stop adding to the backlog: make accessible export the default in the tools faculty and staff use, and teach heading styles and table headers. Our Word-to-PDF guide shows the steps.
  2. Fix the forms and service documents first: admissions, financial aid, registration, housing and accommodation forms.
  3. Inventory the rest by unit, including separate systems such as the catalog, board portal and document repositories. Our inventory guide walks through it.
  4. Budget remediation from real prices: public contract rates for ordinary pages cluster around $3.50 to $6 per page; see what PDF remediation costs.
