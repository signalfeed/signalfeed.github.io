Ohio: Title II PDF backlog at public universities

Data · Last reviewed November 2026 · Sources linked inline

Ohio has 18 public 4-year universities with 5,000+ students in the federal IPEDS directory. Public universities are covered by the ADA Title II web rule (28 CFR part 35, subpart H), and most are part of state government, which puts them on the April 26, 2027 compliance date. Web content, PDFs included, must conform to WCAG 2.1 Level AA, subject to the rule's exceptions.

We measured the PDFs these institutions link from their main websites. This page summarizes Ohio as a whole. It does not name or rank individual institutions. See the national university page for the method.

Ohio at a glance

  • 7 of 18 institutions measured with a usable sample (20 or more PDFs). The rest blocked automated visitors, host documents on other systems, or had too few PDFs to sample; that is not a clean result.
  • 375 PDFs analyzed. 26.7% had no accessibility tags, close to the national figure of 26.7%. 5.9% were scanned images with no usable text, and 30.4% did not declare a document language.
  • 0 of 21 sampled PDFs passed the veraPDF PDF/UA-1 checks.
  • The median measured institution has roughly 486 pages of PDFs failing at least one basic check; across the measured institutions that is on the order of 85,000 pages.
  • 49% of the sampled PDFs were modified in 2024 or later. Recent documents are more likely to still be in active use, and documents used to apply for, access or participate in a service are not covered by the rule's exception for preexisting documents.

What this means for a Ohio university

An untagged PDF gives a screen reader no headings, no reading order and no table structure. A scanned PDF gives it nothing at all. Both typically fail WCAG 2.1 AA (for example 1.3.1 Info and Relationships, and for scans 1.1.1 Non-text Content), and both are common in catalogs, policies, forms and board materials.

Not every old PDF has to be fixed. The rule exempts archived web content and preexisting conventional electronic documents unless they are currently used to apply for, access or participate in a service. The practical order is usually: fix the export settings so new documents are born accessible, remediate forms and anything people use to get a service, and move true archives to a clearly marked archive section. Our ADA Title II PDF guide walks through it.

What this is, and what it isn't

These are automated signals, not a conformance evaluation. An automated check can show that a PDF fails; it cannot show that a PDF conforms. Estimates are scaled from samples, and a few very long documents can move a number a lot. The full method is on the national Backlog Index page.
