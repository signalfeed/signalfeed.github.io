Title II PDF Backlog Index: US state government websites

Data · Last reviewed November 2026 · Sources linked inline

Every state government is a Title II public entity, and all of them reach the ADA Title II web rule's compliance date on April 26, 2027 (28 CFR part 35, subpart H): web content, including PDFs, must conform to WCAG 2.1 Level AA, subject to the rule's exceptions. We measured what that means for the documents state agencies, courts and legislatures publish on their .gov websites: reports, forms, rules, budgets and notices. For cities and counties, see the city and county editions.

What we found

  • 279 of 682 state-government sites measured (the rest blocked automated visitors, host their documents elsewhere, or had too few PDFs to sample; they are reported as "insufficient sample", never as clean).
  • 14,337 PDFs analyzed. 37.7% were untagged, which means a screen reader gets no structure at all, and 9.5% were scanned, image-only pages with no usable text.
  • 3 of 835 sampled PDFs passed the veraPDF PDF/UA-1 checks.
  • New documents too: of 5,766 sampled PDFs last modified in 2025 or 2026, 27.3% were untagged. Setting up accessible export now helps keep the backlog from growing.
  • The median measured site has roughly 908 pages of PDFs failing at least one basic check (untagged, no document language, or scanned). Across all measured sites that is on the order of 2,300,000 pages. This is a sample-based estimate, and a few very long documents can inflate an individual site's number.

What this is, and what it isn't

These are automated signals, not a conformance evaluation. An automated check can show that a PDF definitely fails (for example, it has no tags). It can never show that a PDF conforms: that takes human review, including with assistive technology. Treat the numbers as the size of the problem, not as a verdict on any agency.

Not every PDF has to be fixed. The rule has exceptions for archived web content and for preexisting conventional electronic documents, unless they are currently used to apply for, access or participate in a service. We record how recently each document was modified so that this can be judged; the estimates above do not subtract exempt documents.

Coverage is partial: we measured state-government sites on .gov domains only. Agencies on other addresses (for example state.xx.us sites) and documents in separate systems (rulemaking, records or court portals) are not counted, so state totals are a floor.

Method

  • Targets: the .gov domains registered to states and territories in CISA's public .gov registry (1,494 domains), probed for a working site and merged where several domains lead to the same site (682 sites).
  • Crawler: obeys robots.txt and crawl delays, makes at most one request per second per site, identifies itself honestly, never logs in, and stops at any bot challenge. It reads up to 150 pages and the site's sitemaps, then samples up to 60 PDFs per site.
  • Checks per PDF: tags (structure tree), document language, title, scanned/image-only pages, page count and modification date. A small sample per site also gets veraPDF PDF/UA-1 validation.
  • Estimated pages are scaled from the sample to all PDF links found on the pages we could reach. Sites that keep documents on separate systems are undercounted.
  • Data as of 2026-11-26. We don't collect personal information; contact emails are not stored.
