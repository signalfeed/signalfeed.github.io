State agencies and the April 2027 ADA Title II deadline: what to do about PDFs

Regulation · Last reviewed November 2026 · Sources linked inline

State agencies, boards, commissions, courts and legislatures are public entities under Title II of the ADA. The Department of Justice's 2024 web rule (28 CFR part 35, subpart H) treats every state government as a large entity: state governments have until April 26, 2027 to make their web content and mobile apps conform to WCAG 2.1 Level AA. (The DOJ moved the original dates back by one year in April 2026.)

What counts

The rule covers web content an agency provides or makes available, directly or through contractors. For most agencies the largest share is documents: forms and applications, rules and guidance, reports to the legislature, budgets and audits, meeting materials for boards and commissions, permits, notices and data releases. PDFs and Office files on the agency site count, and so do documents on separate portals the agency uses to publish them.

There are exceptions. The two that matter most for documents are archived web content (created before the compliance date, kept only for reference, research or recordkeeping, not changed after archiving, and stored in a clearly marked archive area) and preexisting conventional electronic documents posted before the compliance date, unless they are currently used to apply for, access or participate in the agency's programs. A current application form is in scope however old it is. Read the rule's text or ask counsel about edge cases; this page is not legal advice.

Where state sites stand

We measured the PDFs on 279 state-government websites reached from the .gov domains registered to states and territories. About 38% of the sampled PDFs had no accessibility tags at all, about 10% were scanned images with no usable text, and only 3 of 835 sampled PDFs passed a veraPDF PDF/UA-1 check. Of the sampled PDFs last changed in 2025 or 2026, about 27% were still untagged, so new documents are adding to the backlog. State-by-state numbers are on the state government Backlog Index. These are automated signals, not conformance findings.

A practical order of work

  1. Stop adding to the backlog. Set accessible export as the default in the tools staff use (Word's "Document structure tags for accessibility", or the equivalent in other tools), with heading styles, real lists and table header rows. Our Word-to-PDF guide shows the steps.
  2. Fix what people use to get services. Forms, applications, eligibility rules and current guidance come first.
  3. Decide what is archive. Move true archives to clearly labelled archive sections and retire documents nobody needs.
  4. Budget the rest. Public contract prices for remediation cluster around $3.50 to $6 per ordinary page; forms, complex layouts and scans cost more. See what PDF remediation costs. If an agency needs its own numbers for a budget request or RFP, a sizing of its site's PDFs gives the count, page range and per-file detail.
