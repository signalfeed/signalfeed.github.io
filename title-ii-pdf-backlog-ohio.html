Ohio: Title II PDF backlog in cities of 50,000+

Data · Last reviewed November 2026 · Sources linked inline

Ohio has 13 cities of 50,000 or more residents with an official website listed in Wikidata. Under the ADA Title II web rule (28 CFR part 35, subpart H), cities of 50,000 or more must make web content, PDFs included, conform to WCAG 2.1 Level AA by April 26, 2027; smaller Ohio cities and special districts have until April 26, 2028.

Does ADA Title II apply to Ohio?

Yes. Title II is federal law and covers Ohio's state agencies, counties, cities, towns, school districts and special districts; the PDF guide covers the rule's exceptions. This is general information, not legal advice.

We measured the PDFs these cities link from their public websites. This page summarizes Ohio as a whole. It does not name or rank individual cities.

Ohio at a glance

  • 7 of 13 cities measured with a usable sample (20 or more PDFs); together they are home to about 1.3 million residents. Of the rest, 0 blocked our crawler and 6 had too few PDFs on their main site to sample (some host documents on other systems). That is not a clean result.
  • 294 PDFs analyzed. 40.8% had no accessibility tags, close to the national figure of 40.0%. 15.6% were scanned images with no usable text, 46.6% did not declare a document language, and 54% had no document title.
  • Spread: in 2 of the 7 measured cities more than half the sampled PDFs were untagged; in none, fewer than 1 in 10 were. The most common main issue was missing accessibility tags (3 of 7 cities).
  • 0 of 21 sampled PDFs passed the veraPDF PDF/UA-1 checks.
  • The median measured city has roughly 1,700 pages of PDFs failing at least one basic check; across the measured cities that is on the order of 18,000 pages.
  • 48% of the sampled PDFs were modified in 2024 or later. Recent documents are more likely to still be in active use, and documents used to apply for, access or participate in a service are not covered by the rule's exception for preexisting documents.

What this means for a Ohio city

An untagged PDF gives a screen reader no headings, reading order or table structure, and a scanned one gives it nothing; both typically fail WCAG 2.1 AA. Archived content and preexisting documents are exempt unless people use them to get a service, so the usual order is: fix export settings, remediate forms and service documents, then move true archives to a marked archive section.

For agendas, minutes and notices, our accessible meeting templates for small public bodies ($24, Word + PDF/UA) export tagged PDFs from Word, so new documents start out accessible.

What this is, and what it isn't

These are automated signals scaled from samples, not a conformance evaluation: a check can show a PDF fails, not that it conforms. Method: national Backlog Index page.
