Title II PDF Backlog Index: US cities of 50,000+

Data · Last reviewed November 2026 · Sources linked inline

On April 26, 2027, every US city with 50,000 or more residents (in the most recent decennial Census) reaches its compliance date under the ADA Title II web rule (28 CFR part 35, subpart H): web content, including PDFs, must conform to WCAG 2.1 Level AA, subject to the rule's exceptions. We measured what that means for the documents these cities actually publish. Counties are covered in the county edition.

What we found

  • 253 of 657 cities measured (the rest blocked automated visitors, host their documents elsewhere, or had too few PDFs to sample; they are reported as "insufficient sample", never as clean).
  • 11,839 PDFs analyzed. 39.5% were untagged, which means a screen reader gets no structure at all, and 14.2% were scanned, image-only pages with no usable text.
  • 6 of 758 sampled PDFs passed the veraPDF PDF/UA-1 checks.
  • New documents too: of 5,317 sampled PDFs last modified in 2025 or 2026, 29.1% were untagged. Setting up accessible export now helps keep the backlog from growing.
  • The median measured city has roughly 2,376 pages of PDFs failing at least one basic check (untagged, no document language, or scanned). Across all measured cities that is on the order of 1,700,000 pages. This is a sample-based estimate, and a few very long documents can inflate an individual city's number.

What this is, and what it isn't

These are automated signals, not a conformance evaluation. An automated check can show that a PDF definitely fails (for example, it has no tags). It can never show that a PDF conforms: that takes human review, including with assistive technology. Treat the numbers as the size of the problem, not as a verdict on any city.

Not every PDF has to be fixed. The rule has exceptions for archived web content and for preexisting conventional electronic documents, unless they are currently used to apply for, access or participate in a service. We record how recently each document was modified so that this can be judged; the estimates above do not subtract exempt documents.

We crawl each city's own website only. In a separate check of the same cities' homepages and agenda pages, 47.1% of the cities we could visit (170 of 361; the rest disallow automated visitors in robots.txt or didn't respond) linked to a separate agenda or document system such as Legistar, CivicClerk, Granicus, PrimeGov or Laserfiche. PDFs hosted there are not in these counts, so for those cities the backlog shown is a floor.

Method

  • Targets: US incorporated places with 50,000+ residents that have an official website listed in Wikidata (657 cities).
  • Crawler: obeys robots.txt and crawl delays, makes at most one request per second per site, identifies itself honestly, never logs in, and stops at any bot challenge. It reads up to 150 pages and the site's sitemaps, then samples up to 60 PDFs per city.
  • Checks per PDF: tags (structure tree), document language, title, scanned/image-only pages, page count and modification date. A small sample per city also gets veraPDF PDF/UA-1 validation.
  • Estimated pages are scaled from the sample to all PDF links found on the pages we could reach. Cities that keep documents on separate systems are undercounted.
  • Data as of 2026-11-19, refreshed monthly. We don't collect personal information; contact emails are not stored.
