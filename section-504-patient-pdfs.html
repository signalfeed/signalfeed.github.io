HHS Section 504 and your patient PDFs: the 2027 deadline and a checklist

Regulation · Last reviewed November 2026 · Sources linked inline

In 2024 the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services updated its Section 504 rule (45 CFR part 84). For programs and activities receiving HHS federal financial assistance, the rule specifies WCAG 2.1 Level AA for covered web content and mobile apps, subject to its exceptions. (A separate provision prohibits disability discrimination in services provided through kiosks.) Covered web content can include patient-facing documents posted online: intake and registration forms, consent forms, patient handouts, price lists, notices and newsletters.

General information, not legal advice. Whether and how the rule applies to you depends on the funding you receive; ask your compliance officer or attorney.

The dates

In May 2026 HHS issued an interim final rule pushing the compliance dates back one year. The standard didn't change.

Your general duty not to discriminate, including providing documents in an accessible format when a patient asks, applies now.

Who is typically affected

Recipients of HHS funding commonly include hospitals, community and federally qualified health centers, nursing and long-term care facilities, home health and hospice agencies, behavioral health providers, Medicaid providers, and state and local health departments. Check your own funding sources to confirm. A recipient can receive assistance directly or through another recipient; the ultimate beneficiary of assistance is excluded from the definition (45 CFR 84.10). Treating patients does not by itself make a clinic covered; the specific HHS funding relationship controls.

Which documents to fix first

  1. Forms patients need to get care: registration, intake, health history, consent, financial assistance and records-request forms.
  2. Information patients rely on: visit instructions, prep sheets, discharge and medication handouts, notices of privacy practices, price transparency documents.
  3. Everything new. Fix the Word templates your staff use, so new PDFs come out tagged.

Exceptions, and their limits

Section 84.85 includes exceptions for qualifying archived content, preexisting conventional electronic documents, certain third-party content, secured individualized documents and preexisting social-media posts. A preexisting document is not exempt when it is currently used to apply for, access or participate in your services: an old intake form still in use should not be treated as exempt just because it is old.

“Archived” means all of these: created before the applicable date (or reproducing older physical media), kept only for reference, research or recordkeeping, not altered after archiving, and stored in a dedicated, clearly identified archive area (84.10). An old date or an “archive” label alone isn't enough.

Checklist for each PDF

  • Tagged PDF with headings, lists and tables marked up, and a logical reading order.
  • Document title and language set.
  • Alt text on informative images and diagrams (for example a dosing chart or a map to the clinic).
  • Form fields labeled, in a logical tab order, and fillable with a keyboard; or an accessible web form instead.
  • Enough color contrast; no meaning carried by color alone.
  • Scanned forms re-created as real text, not images.

The free quick check looks for basic structural warning signs (it runs in your browser and never uploads the file). It can't confirm reading order, table relationships or whether descriptions are right; those need a person and a screen reader.
